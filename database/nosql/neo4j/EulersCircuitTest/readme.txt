The Criterion for Euler Paths

If a graph G has an Euler path, then it must have
exactly two odd vertices.

If the number of odd vertices in G is anything other
than 2, then G cannot have an Euler path


The Criterion for Euler Circuits

If a graph G has an Euler circuit, then all of its vertices
must be even vertices.


If the number of odd vertices in G is anything other
than 0, then G cannot have an Euler circuit

History of Seven Bridges of Königsberg
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Bridges_of_K%C3%B6nigsberg

The city of Königsberg in Prussia (now Kaliningrad, Russia) was set on both sides of the Pregel River, and included two large islands—Kneiphof and Lomse—which were connected to each other, and to the two mainland portions of the city—Altstadt and Vorstadt—by seven bridges. The problem was to devise a walk through the city that would cross each of those bridges once and only once. 

The Seven Bridges of Königsberg is a historically notable problem in mathematics. Its negative resolution by Leonhard Euler, in 1736,[1] laid the foundations of graph theory and prefigured the idea of topology.[2]
